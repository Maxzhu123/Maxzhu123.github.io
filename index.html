About

I am a PhD student in the Statistical Laboratory, part of the Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics at the University of Cambridge. My research is in machine learning, spanning causal inference, foundation models, physics systems and training optimization.

I have also worked on machine learning for scientific discovery. My first paper used Bayesian active learning to map complex phase diagrams, and I have built numerical solvers and learned surrogates for fluid dynamics.

Research

I am interested in machine learning methodology: how models are built, when they can be trusted, and what guarantees we can give for them. My current work is supervised by Qingyuan Zhao.

I also work on machine learning for the physical sciences, where the data is expensive: active learning to design the next experiment, and learned surrogates for numerical simulation.

Projects

  • Sparse activations in training

    A training-time codec that keeps only the nonzero activations behind a ReLU feed-forward layer, stored as a packed bitmask with tile offsets and reconstructed during the backward pass. Supports BF16 and FP8 storage, squared ReLU and optional sign-bit packing, with Triton kernels benchmarked against CSR, nvCOMP and dense compaction on nanoGPT and Nemotron-H.

    Code

  • Lossless tensor compression

    A compressed tensor type with GPU-friendly codecs, so tensors stay compressed in memory and are decompressed on the fly during training. Covers matmul, pointwise and sparse-update paths, plugs into nanoGPT and Qwen3 training, and includes measurements of how compressible language model weights and activations actually are.

    Code

  • Active learning for phase diagrams

    Bayesian active learning with Gaussian processes that picks the most informative experiment to run next, mapping multi-phase diagrams in two and three dimensions from a small fraction of the possible sample points. Includes a 2D/3D viewer.

    Code

  • Finite-volume solver

    A compressible finite-volume solver with Newtonian, power-law, Carreau and Herschel–Bulkley viscosity models, isentropic, characteristic and farfield boundary conditions, mesh generation for nozzle and ellipse geometries, and CUDA benchmarks in two and three dimensions.

    Code
